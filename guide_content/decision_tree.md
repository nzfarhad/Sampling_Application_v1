# Decision Tree

The Decision Tree recommends one sampling design for your study, explains why, and sends its settings to the calculators and the Sample Generator. It is the place to start a new study.

## How to Use It

1. **Say what the study is for.** Nothing is shown until you answer this first question. The choices are:
   - measuring a level (PDM, coverage, satisfaction);
   - measuring change or impact;
   - verifying sites or beneficiaries;
   - understanding why or how (KIIs, FGDs);
   - piloting a tool.
2. **Answer the questions that appear.** Only the questions that matter for your purpose are shown:
   - what you are sampling;
   - what list you have;
   - what results must be reported for (tick every group, e.g. province and gender);
   - how spread out the units are;
   - how you will interview;
   - which constraints apply (tick all that apply).

   For studies that measure change, three more questions cover the comparison group, whether the same households are followed, and whether areas were assigned at random.
3. **Read the recommendation.** It updates as you answer and shows:
   - **How to select the sample:** each stage, e.g. strata, then villages by PPS, then households from the list.
   - **Why this design:** the answers that led to it.
   - **Alternatives, and when to switch:** what to do if the field situation turns out differently.
   - **Points to watch:** coverage gaps, insecure areas, reaching women, and the limits of the design.
   - **Field protocols:** links to the Sampling Guideline pages to follow, such as the Replacement Protocol.
4. **Go to the next step.**
   - **Calculate sample size** opens the Sample Calculator, or the Evaluation Calculator for studies of change, with the method and a starting design effect filled in.
   - **Draw the sample** opens the Sample Generator with the method set, and tells you which strata column to choose after uploading the frame.

   A note at the top of the receiving tab lists what was filled in. Check the values against your study, and change any of them.
5. **Download the report** as a PDF for the study file. It always matches the answers on screen.

## How the Recommendations Work

- **No random selection:** pilots and qualitative work get a non-probability design (purposive, purposive across groups, or snowball for a group with no list). Their results describe the people interviewed, not the population.
- **Groups that cannot be listed:** for a survey, the tree suggests building a list first or respondent-driven sampling.
- **Verification:** with a few sites, visit every site. Otherwise, select a random sample of sites, stratified by the reporting groups.
- **Phone:** with a list, numbers are drawn from it. Without a list, random digit dialling is suggested, with its coverage limits in Afghanistan. Mixed mode adds in-person visits for people without a phone.
- **In person:**
  - A few reachable sites: every site is included, with a random sample in each.
  - A full list with no security or budget limits: units are drawn directly from the list (stratified when results are reported by group).
  - Otherwise: a two-stage cluster sample, stratified when results are reported by group. Villages are selected by PPS when counts are available.
- **Gender** can only be a stratum when it is recorded on the unit list. Otherwise the tree explains how to interview women within selected households.
- **Studies of change** also get the matching evaluation design: two independent groups, panel, or difference-in-differences. With a comparison group, the two groups are sampled separately.

The recommendation is a starting point. Consult a sampling specialist for complex studies.
