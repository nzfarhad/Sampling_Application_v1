# Decision Tree

The Decision Tree suggests sampling methods from six questions about your study.

## How to Use It

1. **Answer the questions:** survey mode, whether results must be generalised, subgroup comparisons, what list (frame) you have, how many communities are involved, and your main constraint.
2. **Click Analyze Requirements.** The recommended methods appear with their requirements, pros and cons.
3. **Read "Points to consider".** It lists caveats for your answers, for example a coverage warning when the frame is incomplete, or the limits of random digit dialing in Afghanistan.
4. **Download the report** as a PDF for the study file.

## How the Recommendations Work

- If results do not need to be generalised, only non-probability methods are suggested (purposive or quota). Snowball sampling is suggested only for hidden groups that have no list.
- If you can visit every community, the tree suggests including all sites and sampling within each one.
- Remote or insecure areas, or more than about 70 communities, point to cluster designs.
- Subgroup comparisons point to stratified designs.

The recommendations are indicative. Consult a sampling specialist for complex studies.
