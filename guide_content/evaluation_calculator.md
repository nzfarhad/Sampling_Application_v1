# Evaluation Calculator

Sample size, or the smallest detectable difference, when comparing a proportion between groups or survey rounds.

## Designs

- **Two independent groups:** treatment and control at one time, or a baseline and an endline that each draw a new sample.
- **Panel:** the same households at baseline and endline. Set the baseline-endline correlation (ρ; use 0 if unknown) and the expected attrition.
- **Difference-in-differences:** treatment and control areas at baseline and endline, as repeated cross-sections or as a panel. Enter both baselines and the change expected without the programme.

## What to Enter

- Power (default 80%), significance level (default 5%) and a one- or two-sided test.
- The baseline proportion and the detectable difference D (default 0.10, i.e. 10 percentage points), as an increase or a decrease. You can also type the second proportion directly.
- The response rate, and for clustered samples the interviews per cluster (m) and the ICC.
- The allocation ratio, if one group should be larger (e.g. 2 control households per treatment household).

## Two Modes

- **Sample size:** the sample per group, clusters per group and total interviews needed.
- **Minimum detectable difference (MDE):** enter the clusters (or interviews) per group your budget allows, and see the smallest difference you can detect.

## Results and Downloads

The power curve shows how power changes with the sample size, or with the true difference in MDE mode. The Excel download holds the full calculation, the group table and the curve.
