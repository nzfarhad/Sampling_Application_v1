# Case Studies

## National Health Surveys

### Case Study 1: National Health and Nutrition Examination Survey (NHANES)

#### **Background**
The National Health and Nutrition Examination Survey (NHANES) is a program of studies designed to assess the health and nutritional status of adults and children in the United States.

#### **Sampling Design**
- **Population**: Non-institutionalized civilian population of the United States
- **Sampling Method**: Complex multistage probability sampling
- **Stages**:
  1. Primary Sampling Units (PSUs): Counties or groups of counties
  2. Secondary Sampling Units: Census blocks or block groups
  3. Tertiary Sampling Units: Households
  4. Final Sampling Units: Individuals within households

#### **Key Features**
- **Oversampling**: Certain demographic groups (e.g., African Americans, Hispanics, elderly)
- **Rotation**: Continuous data collection with annual releases
- **Sample Size**: Approximately 5,000 individuals per year
- **Response Rate**: 70-80% for household interviews, 60-70% for medical examinations

#### **Challenges and Solutions**
- **Challenge**: Low response rates for medical examinations
- **Solution**: Incentives, flexible scheduling, mobile examination centers
- **Challenge**: Geographic dispersion
- **Solution**: Mobile examination centers, local partnerships

#### **Lessons Learned**
- Oversampling improves precision for subgroups
- Mobile examination centers increase participation
- Incentives significantly improve response rates
- Community partnerships enhance trust and participation

### Case Study 2: Demographic and Health Surveys (DHS)

#### **Background**
The Demographic and Health Surveys (DHS) Program provides data for policy and program decisions in developing countries.

#### **Sampling Design**
- **Population**: Women aged 15-49 and men aged 15-59
- **Sampling Method**: Two-stage stratified cluster sampling
- **Stages**:
  1. Enumeration Areas (EAs): Census enumeration areas
  2. Households: Systematic selection within EAs
- **Stratification**: Urban/rural, administrative regions

#### **Key Features**
- **Sample Size**: 5,000-30,000 households per country
- **Response Rate**: 90%+ for household interviews, 95%+ for individual interviews
- **Periodicity**: Every 3-5 years per country
- **Coverage**: 90+ countries

#### **Challenges and Solutions**
- **Challenge**: Geographic accessibility
- **Solution**: Local enumerators, community engagement
- **Challenge**: Cultural sensitivity
- **Solution**: Gender-matched interviewers, cultural training
- **Challenge**: Data quality
- **Solution**: Supervisory visits, data validation

#### **Lessons Learned**
- Local enumerators improve response rates
- Cultural sensitivity training is essential
- Supervisory visits maintain data quality
- Community engagement builds trust

## Agricultural Surveys

### Case Study 3: Agricultural Resource Management Survey (ARMS)

#### **Background**
The Agricultural Resource Management Survey (ARMS) is the primary source of information on the financial condition, production practices, and resource use of American farms.

#### **Sampling Design**
- **Population**: Farm operations in the United States
- **Sampling Method**: Stratified random sampling
- **Stratification**: Farm size, type, region
- **Sample Size**: 30,000+ farms annually

#### **Key Features**
- **Multi-phase**: Screening survey followed by detailed survey
- **Rotation**: Different commodities surveyed each year
- **Integration**: Links with Census of Agriculture
- **Response Rate**: 60-70%

#### **Challenges and Solutions**
- **Challenge**: Farm definition changes
- **Solution**: Consistent farm definition criteria
- **Challenge**: Seasonal timing
- **Solution**: Flexible data collection periods
- **Challenge**: Complex farm operations
- **Solution**: Trained enumerators, detailed protocols

#### **Lessons Learned**
- Consistent definitions are crucial
- Flexible timing improves data quality
- Trained enumerators handle complex operations
- Integration with other surveys enhances efficiency

### Case Study 4: Crop Production Surveys

#### **Background**
Crop production surveys estimate agricultural production for major crops in various countries.

#### **Sampling Design**
- **Population**: Agricultural fields or farms
- **Sampling Method**: Area frame sampling with list frame supplementation
- **Frame**: Satellite imagery and administrative records
- **Sample Size**: Varies by crop and country

#### **Key Features**
- **Remote Sensing**: Satellite imagery for frame development
- **Ground Truth**: Field visits for validation
- **Timing**: Multiple visits during growing season
- **Integration**: Combines multiple data sources

#### **Challenges and Solutions**
- **Challenge**: Field access
- **Solution**: Landowner permission, flexible scheduling
- **Challenge**: Weather conditions
- **Solution**: Multiple visit windows, weather monitoring
- **Challenge**: Crop identification
- **Solution**: Expert enumerators, reference materials

#### **Lessons Learned**
- Remote sensing enhances frame quality
- Multiple visits improve accuracy
- Expert enumerators are essential
- Weather flexibility is crucial

## Business Surveys

### Case Study 5: Business Register Surveys

#### **Background**
Business register surveys collect information on business characteristics, employment, and economic activity.

#### **Sampling Design**
- **Population**: Businesses in business register
- **Sampling Method**: Stratified random sampling
- **Stratification**: Industry, size, region
- **Sample Size**: 50,000+ businesses annually

#### **Key Features**
- **Register-based**: Uses administrative business register
- **Stratification**: By industry, employment size, region
- **Rotation**: Annual rotation of sample
- **Response Rate**: 80-90%

#### **Challenges and Solutions**
- **Challenge**: Business register quality
- **Solution**: Regular register updates, validation
- **Challenge**: Business closures
- **Solution**: Rapid frame updates, replacement procedures
- **Challenge**: Response burden
- **Solution**: Streamlined questionnaires, online collection

#### **Lessons Learned**
- Register quality affects survey quality
- Rapid updates maintain frame currency
- Streamlined questionnaires improve response
- Online collection reduces burden

## Social Surveys

### Case Study 7: General Social Survey (GSS)

#### **Background**
The General Social Survey (GSS) monitors societal change and studies the growing complexity of American society.

#### **Sampling Design**
- **Population**: Non-institutionalized adults in the United States
- **Sampling Method**: Two-stage area probability sampling
- **Stages**:
  1. Primary Sampling Units: Counties or groups of counties
  2. Secondary Sampling Units: Census blocks
- **Sample Size**: 2,000+ adults annually

#### **Key Features**
- **Longitudinal**: Some questions asked repeatedly
- **Cross-sectional**: New samples each year
- **Response Rate**: 60-70%
- **Duration**: Since 1972

#### **Challenges and Solutions**
- **Challenge**: Declining response rates
- **Solution**: Incentives, flexible scheduling, multiple contact attempts
- **Challenge**: Question relevance
- **Solution**: Regular question review, pilot testing
- **Challenge**: Mode effects
- **Solution**: Consistent methodology, validation

#### **Lessons Learned**
- Declining response rates require adaptation
- Question relevance affects response quality
- Consistent methodology maintains comparability
- Multiple contact attempts improve response

### Case Study 8: Crime Victimization Surveys

#### **Background**
Crime victimization surveys measure the extent and nature of crime in society.

#### **Sampling Design**
- **Population**: Households and individuals
- **Sampling Method**: Stratified multistage cluster sampling
- **Stratification**: Urban/rural, region
- **Sample Size**: 100,000+ households annually

#### **Key Features**
- **Household-based**: Interviews with household members
- **Sensitive Topics**: Special protocols for sensitive questions
- **Response Rate**: 80-90%
- **Frequency**: Annual or biennial

#### **Challenges and Solutions**
- **Challenge**: Sensitive topics
- **Solution**: Trained interviewers, privacy protocols
- **Challenge**: Memory effects
- **Solution**: Short reference periods, memory aids
- **Challenge**: Underreporting
- **Solution**: Anonymous reporting options, trust building

#### **Lessons Learned**
- Sensitive topics require special protocols
- Short reference periods improve accuracy
- Trust building reduces underreporting
- Trained interviewers handle sensitive topics

### Case Study 10: Programme for International Student Assessment (PISA)

#### **Background**
PISA assesses the knowledge and skills of 15-year-old students in reading, mathematics, and science.

#### **Sampling Design**
- **Population**: 15-year-old students in participating countries
- **Sampling Method**: Two-stage stratified sampling
- **Stages**:
  1. Schools: Stratified by school type, region
  2. Students: Random selection within schools
- **Sample Size**: 5,000-10,000 students per country

#### **Key Features**
- **International**: Harmonized across countries
- **Student Assessment**: Direct assessment of skills
- **Response Rate**: 80-95%
- **Coverage**: 80+ countries

#### **Challenges and Solutions**
- **Challenge**: School participation
- **Solution**: Incentives, flexible scheduling
- **Challenge**: Student participation
- **Solution**: Parental consent, student incentives
- **Challenge**: Cultural adaptation
- **Solution**: Local adaptation, validation

#### **Lessons Learned**
- School participation requires incentives
- Parental consent is crucial
- Cultural adaptation maintains validity
- Local validation ensures quality

## Lessons Learned Across Case Studies

### Common Success Factors

1. **Clear Objectives**: Well-defined survey objectives guide design decisions
2. **Quality Frames**: High-quality sampling frames are essential
3. **Appropriate Methods**: Sampling methods must match objectives and constraints
4. **Response Strategies**: Multiple strategies needed to maintain response rates
5. **Quality Control**: Continuous monitoring and quality control essential
6. **Local Partnerships**: Local partnerships improve implementation
7. **Cultural Sensitivity**: Cultural adaptation improves data quality
8. **Resource Planning**: Adequate resources ensure quality implementation

### Common Challenges

1. **Declining Response Rates**: Universal challenge requiring adaptation
2. **Frame Quality**: Maintaining current, complete frames
3. **Resource Constraints**: Balancing quality with available resources
4. **Cultural Differences**: Adapting to local contexts
5. **Sensitive Topics**: Handling sensitive questions appropriately
6. **Technology Changes**: Adapting to new technologies
7. **Coordination**: Managing complex, multi-country surveys
8. **Quality Assurance**: Maintaining quality across diverse contexts

### Best Practices

1. **Pilot Testing**: Always pilot test new procedures
2. **Training**: Invest in comprehensive training
3. **Monitoring**: Continuous monitoring of implementation
4. **Adaptation**: Be prepared to adapt to local conditions
5. **Documentation**: Thorough documentation of procedures
6. **Evaluation**: Regular evaluation of survey performance
7. **Innovation**: Embrace new technologies and methods
8. **Collaboration**: Work with local partners and stakeholders

## Conclusion

These case studies demonstrate the diversity of sampling applications and the importance of adapting methods to specific contexts. While each survey faces unique challenges, common themes emerge around the importance of quality frames, appropriate methods, response strategies, and cultural sensitivity.

The key to successful sampling lies not just in choosing the right method, but in implementing it with attention to local context, quality control, and continuous improvement. By learning from these case studies and applying their lessons, researchers can improve their own sampling practices and contribute to the advancement of survey methodology.
